SWP Formula

SWP balances with periodic (monthly) withdrawals follow a linear recurrence that can be expressed in closed form for steady withdrawals and constant periodic return.

Formula (mathematical):

Bn = C × (1 + r)n − W × ( ( (1 + r)n − 1 ) / r )

Where:

  • C = Initial corpus (starting balance)
  • W = Periodic withdrawal amount (monthly)
  • r = Periodic rate (monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = Number of periods (months)
  • Bn = Remaining balance after n periods

If r is effectively zero the formula reduces to Bn = C − W × n. This calculator uses a month-by-month iterative simulation (adding returns, subtracting withdrawals) which is numerically equivalent to the closed-form expression while avoiding small rounding differences for fractional years.

This SWP calculator simulates regular withdrawals from an initial corpus while the remaining corpus continues to earn the expected annual return. It models the practical flow: each month the corpus accrues investment return (converted to a monthly rate) and then the withdrawal is made. Use it to plan retirement income, pension supplements, or any scenario where you need predictable periodic cash flows from a lump-sum.

When to use this calculator

Use it when you want to know how long a corpus will last at a chosen withdrawal rate, how much you will have left after a set period, or to compare withdrawal amounts that preserve capital versus those that deplete it over time. It’s useful for retirees, investors drawing down portfolios, and financial planning scenarios requiring regular income from capital.

How it works (plain explanation)

The calculator converts the annual expected return into a monthly rate. For every simulated month it first applies the monthly return to the current balance and then subtracts the withdrawal amount. This order mirrors many SWP implementations where growth is credited before the payout. The tool totals withdrawals, tracks remaining balance each year for visualization, and flags if the corpus depletes before the chosen duration.

Benefits of a well-planned SWP

An SWP provides predictable periodic income while keeping the corpus invested — potentially allowing your capital to continue earning returns. Compared to withdrawing ad-hoc, SWP can provide discipline and smoother cash flows. Properly sized withdrawals can preserve capital for longer, while larger withdrawals deliver more immediate income but increase the risk of depletion.

Factors that affect SWP outcomes

Key factors include initial corpus size, withdrawal amount, expected average return, and tenure. Higher returns and lower withdrawal rates increase longevity. Inflation, taxes on withdrawals, and unexpected market volatility also affect real purchasing power and sustainability — so use conservative return estimates and revisit assumptions regularly.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I withdraw monthly? That depends on your corpus, expected returns, and desired longevity. Use conservative return assumptions to avoid depleting capital too quickly.

What if the corpus depletes early? If the simulated balance hits zero before your planned duration, you will not receive further withdrawals unless you reduce withdrawal amounts or add fresh capital.

Should I use expected returns or guaranteed returns? For market-linked portfolios use expected returns (conservative). For guaranteed products use the product’s guaranteed rate. This tool accepts an expected annual return you choose and simulates accordingly.
