When to use this calculator

Use this tool when you are considering a lump-sum prepayment on your home loan and want to compare the two typical outcomes lenders offer: reduced tenure (same EMI) or reduced EMI (same tenure). It helps estimate interest savings and the new repayment profile.

How it works (plain explanation)

The calculator computes an EMI (if you don't supply one) using standard amortisation formulas, finds the outstanding balance at the chosen prepayment month, applies the prepayment to reduce principal, then:

  • Option A — keeps your EMI unchanged and solves for the shorter remaining tenure required to amortise the reduced principal.
  • Option B — keeps the remaining tenure unchanged and recalculates EMI for the lower principal.

Benefits of prepayment

Prepaying reduces outstanding principal and therefore total interest paid. If you have surplus cash and no significant prepayment penalty, you can shorten the loan duration or lower monthly payments.

Factors that affect savings

Interest rate, timing of prepayment (earlier gives larger savings), prepayment penalties, and whether your lender recalculates interest monthly or on specific dates. Also compare alternative uses of cash (investments, emergency buffer) before prepaying.

Frequently asked questions

Will my lender always let me prepay? Most lenders allow prepayment but terms vary — check for charges, minimum prepayment amounts, and any processing rules.

Should I reduce EMI or tenure? Reducing tenure typically saves more interest; reducing EMI may help cash flow. Use this calculator to compare both outcomes.

Does prepayment affect interest already due? Prepayment reduces future interest by lowering principal. Interest already accrued remains payable as per lender rules.
