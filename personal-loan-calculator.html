Personal Loan EMI Formula

EMI for a loan using the reducing-balance method is calculated using the standard formula below.

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n / [ (1 + r)n − 1 ]

Where:

  • P = Loan principal amount
  • r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = Total number of monthly installments (years × 12)

When to use this calculator

Use this calculator before applying for a personal loan to estimate monthly outgoings and total borrowing cost. It's helpful for comparing offers with different tenures or rates, and for understanding how much interest you will pay over time.

How it works (plain explanation)

The tool converts the annual interest rate into a monthly rate and applies the reducing-balance formula to compute the fixed monthly EMI. Each month a portion of the EMI pays interest on the outstanding balance and the remainder reduces the principal. Over time interest component declines and principal component increases. The calculator sums interest across all months to present total interest payable.

Benefits of using the calculator

The calculator gives a clear, comparable view of loan costs so you can make informed choices: choose a tenure that balances manageable EMIs with lower total interest, compare fixed vs floating rates, and factor in one-time charges like processing fees. It also helps with cashflow planning by revealing exact monthly commitments and long-term costs.

Factors that affect loan cost

Key drivers of the total cost are the interest rate, tenure, and any fees charged by the lender (processing fees, prepayment penalties). Lower rates and shorter tenures reduce total interest but increase monthly EMIs. Floating rates can change over time; fixed rates remain constant but may start higher.

Frequently asked questions

Can I prepay a personal loan? Many lenders allow prepayment, but terms and charges vary—check prepayment penalties and whether prepayment reduces EMI or tenure.

What's the difference between fixed and floating rate? Fixed rate stays the same for the agreed period; floating rate moves with market benchmarks and can change EMI or tenure depending on lender rules.

Are processing fees added to the loan? Processing fees are typically charged upfront or deducted from the disbursed amount; they are shown separately here so you see the loan economics clearly.
