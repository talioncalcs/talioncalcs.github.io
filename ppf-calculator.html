PPF Formula

The PPF maturity for regular yearly deposits with annual compounding can be represented using the annuity-due style formula if deposits are considered at the start of each year. Practically, this calculator applies annual compounding iteratively to each yearly deposit for accuracy.

Formula (mathematical, annuity due):

M = P × [ ( (1 + r)t − 1 ) / r ] × (1 + r)

Where:

  • P = Annual deposit amount
  • r = Annual interest rate (decimal, e.g. 7% = 0.07)
  • t = Tenure in years
  • M = Estimated maturity amount

This calculator uses an iterative year-by-year method (deposit + annual interest) to match PPF’s annual crediting and avoid rounding discrepancies.

This PPF calculator projects the future value of annual deposits in a Public Provident Fund by compounding the accumulated balance once per year at the specified rate. It is suitable for planning long-term tax-efficient savings, retirement top-ups, or secure fixed-return investments using the government-backed PPF scheme. When to use this calculator

Use this calculator when you plan to contribute a fixed amount annually to a PPF account and want to estimate how that discipline grows into a retirement corpus or long-term savings target. It helps compare tenors and rates, and to decide whether to increase annual deposits to meet a target maturity amount.

How it works (plain explanation)

The tool treats each yearly contribution as being added to the account and then the full balance grows by the annual interest rate at year end. Earlier deposits compound for more years than later ones, so the tenure strongly affects the final corpus. By iterating year-by-year, the calculator mirrors the way PPF interest is credited and avoids approximation errors from closed-form expressions for staggered deposits.

Benefits of PPF investing

PPF offers tax-free interest and tax benefits on contributions under applicable laws (e.g., Section 80C in India). It is government-backed, provides guaranteed returns (as per declared rates), and suits conservative savers looking for long-term capital preservation with modest growth. Regular yearly contributions also encourage disciplined savings behavior.

Factors that affect PPF returns

The main drivers are the annual deposit amount, the declared PPF interest rate, and the tenure. Since PPF interest rates are reviewed periodically by the government, future rates may change; use conservative estimates when planning. Premature withdrawals, loans against PPF, and tax rules also influence net outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

How often is PPF interest credited? PPF interest is credited annually at the end of the financial year; this calculator uses annual compounding to match that behavior.

Can I deposit monthly into PPF? PPF accepts deposits throughout the year but it is common to consider annual totals for projection; this calculator models equal yearly deposits. For non-uniform deposits, use an iterative manual approach or adapt deposit amounts per year.

Are returns guaranteed? PPF returns are government-backed and the rate announced periodically; while currently guaranteed for the tenor, future rate changes can affect new contributions and projections.
